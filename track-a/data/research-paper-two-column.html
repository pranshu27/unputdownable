Two-Column Layout Robustness in Retrieval-Augmented Generation Pipelines

Abstract. We study how multi-column document layouts degrade naive retrieval pipelines. Across 4,200 synthetic two-column pages, right-half sentences retrieved 31% worse than left-half sentences under single-column chunkers. A column-aware pre-pass recovers 27 of the 31 point gap. We release our corpus and evaluation harness (arXiv identifier 2609.04112, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2609.04112).

1. Introduction

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems are increasingly deployed over heterogeneous document corpora: SEC filings, academic preprints, and scanned contracts. While chunking strategies have received attention, the interplay between physical page layout and logical reading order remains underexplored. A two-column page read left-to-right line-by-line interleaves unrelated sentences, corrupting both dense embeddings and lexical indices.

We make three contributions. First, a 4,200-page synthetic corpus with controlled column offsets (Section 3). Second, a degradation study quantifying column-induced recall loss for dense, sparse, and hybrid retrievers (Section 4). Third, a column-aware pre-pass that reorders blocks into logical reading order before chunking, recovering most of the loss (Section 5).

Figure 1 (caption): Recall@5 by retriever family. Dense-only suffers most: 0.72 left-half vs 0.49 right-half. Hybrid RRF narrows the gap to 0.71 vs 0.61, suggesting sparse signals partially compensate for interleaving artifacts.

2. Related Work

Layout-aware parsing has been studied for tables and forms, with early work on X-Y cut algorithms and recent transformer-based layout models. Our contribution is not a new detector but an end-to-end retrieval measurement: we show that even a perfect layout detector helps retrieval only when its output is enforced before chunk boundaries are drawn.

Closely related are sentence-window retrieval and contextual chunk headers; we treat both as orthogonal and apply them uniformly across all conditions.

3. Corpus Construction

Pages were rendered at 300 DPI from a 6-page HTML template with column counts in {1, 2}, font sizes in {9, 10, 11}pt, and injected distractor passages. Each page carries a synthetic citation key (e.g. [RETR-2023-0447]) placed in exactly one column half, enabling order-aware scoring.

The dense-only gap of 0.23 collapses to 0.04 once the column-aware pre-pass reorders blocks. Notably, hybrid retrieval without the pre-pass still leaves a 0.10 gap, confirming that fusion cannot repair structurally corrupted chunks.

5. Column-Aware Pre-Pass

The pre-pass operates on parser output: it detects vertical whitespace gutters, splits blocks spanning both halves, and emits blocks in left-column-complete-then- right-column order. It adds 6.1ms per page (p95 9.8ms) and requires no model inference, keeping ingest cost unchanged.

6. Limitations and Future Work

Our corpus is synthetic and English-only; real-world multi-column PDFs include figures, pull quotes, and marginalia that may confuse gutter detection. Future work will evaluate on the PubLayNet and DocBank corpora and extend the pre-pass to three-column and sidebar layouts.

7. Reproducibility

Code, corpus, and the evaluation harness are available under internal tracking key RETR-CORPUS-v2. All experiments used a fixed seed of 20260929 and a single CPU-only embedding backend for cost comparability.
